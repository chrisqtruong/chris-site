Swear Jar

A swear jar for my AI habit. It’s a tiny pixel dashboard of where my credits go while I build Vox2, my lightweight desktop translator: day by day, by focus area, and against its roadmap.

Why

where is the effort actually going?

I build Vox2, my lightweight desktop translator, mostly by working with Claude. A product has a lot of sides (the interface, bug fixes, the Mac version, releases, the meaning check), and with an AI partner it’s easy to pour effort into whatever feels most interesting that day.

I wanted to keep the work aligned to the roadmap: spending where the value is, and sticking to the north star of a translator people can trust and reach for every day. Swear Jar sorts each request into a focus area, prices it, and sets the totals next to the roadmap’s priorities, so drift shows up early.

It’s a good guess, not an audit, but it already paid off. It showed the meaning check was taking about half of the roadmap effort while holding about a third of its value, and that everyday features were falling behind. That’s exactly the kind of course correction it’s for.

Private

private by design

Everything is read on my own computer. Only totals per day and focus area are published; never what I asked or what Claude wrote.
