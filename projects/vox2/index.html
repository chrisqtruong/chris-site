01Why

why i built it

I was working on an AI product with a design team, and one of my teammates only spoke Portuguese. Every message between us took a detour: I’d paste mine into Google Translate or an AI app, he’d do the same, then answer in English so I could understand. We were always one browser tab away from actually talking to each other.

I wanted something so light it disappears: a way to break the language barrier in seconds and get back to the conversation. When it first worked, it felt like I’d unlocked another world. That’s the feeling I want Vox2 to give anyone trying to connect with someone across a language.

02Design

small decisions that make it feel calm

New words show grey until the engine is sure, so the text never jumps around while you type.

trust, not just translation

The translation comes back into your language with a score for how much of your meaning survived. It compares meaning, not exact words.

37 themes, readable for everyone

Color is personal, so there are lots of themes. Scores can switch to a colorblind-safe palette with ✓ ! ✕ symbols, so meaning never depends on color alone.

private by default

No account, no server, no analytics. Voice, screen snips and the meaning check run on your own computer.

03Testing

testing my own feature, honestly

A score is only useful if you can trust it, so I test the meaning check like a researcher would: real sentences in 11 languages, with mistakes planted on purpose (a flipped “not”, a changed number, a swapped pronoun), and fresh sentences every round so it can’t just memorize the test.

29% → 79%of meaning errors caught, from the first test to now
94%of good translations shown as “meaning kept”, with false alarms down to 4%
96% → 8%of gibberish passed off as a match; now it says “can’t check” instead

Six test runs, 11 languages, more than 10,000 translations scored. Every run is published with its method and raw results on GitHub.

The first test caught under a third of the mistakes, so I added targeted checks for negations, numbers, pronouns and opposites, and taught it to say “can’t check” when text never got translated, instead of showing a misleading number. Pronouns are still the weakest spot, so translations that get people right, like “they” or “partner”, are what I’m working on next.

The goal behind all of it: when you write to your family, your in-laws or a coworker in their language, you should know they’ll understand what you meant. Language shouldn’t decide who feels close to you, or what you get to read.

04Next

where the testing goes next

The plan is phases, not a big rewrite. Each one is measured on sentences the checks have never seen, and every result gets published, including the ones that don’t move the number.

  1. Beyond English. The checks look hardest at the English side today. Next, the same checks in other languages, so a reply in Vietnamese or Portuguese gets the same scrutiny.
  2. Show everything that changed. When a check flags a problem, Vox2 already underlines the words behind it and lists the ones that didn’t come back. Next: highlight words that were added too, and test whether the highlights actually help people catch mistakes.
  3. A second opinion. Translate back with a different engine, so one engine can’t agree with its own mistake, plus an optional AI double-check for the messages that matter most.
  4. Harder, more realistic tests. Messy text from real life (chat messages, screen snips, names, slang) and the cases it still misses, like pronouns, tested on purpose until they hold up.

All of it has to stay light. The checks run on your own computer, and anything heavier, like the AI double-check, stays optional. The bar isn’t more features. It’s trusting a translation enough to hit send without a second app open.
