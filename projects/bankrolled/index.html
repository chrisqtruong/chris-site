Why

follow the money

I’m constantly impressed by independent projects that keep a public record of corruption, corporate greed and abuse of power, built by people who decided someone should be keeping track. One that stuck with me is Rachel LaPointe’s Gendered Police Violence, where a New York social worker documents gender-based violence by police, case by case. Work like that inspired me to start my own.

I want a fairer, more equitable world, and getting there means watching what large corporations do with their money and power: how they profit from the communities they operate in, and how they buy influence over the people who are supposed to regulate them. Gambling is the first industry Bankrolled tracks.

Sports betting went from illegal in most of the country to an app on everyone’s phone in a few years, and prediction markets like Kalshi and Polymarket now sell sports bets as “contracts”. Bettors lost $17 billion to legal sportsbooks in 2025, nearly four times what they lost in 2021. The companies spend over a billion a year on marketing, sign deals with leagues, teams and star athletes, and funded a $72 million super PAC ahead of the 2026 midterms. Bankrolled treats both the same and puts the whole picture in one place: the political money, the deals, the fines and lawsuits, and what the research says it’s costing people.

Rules

only what can be sourced

Bankrolled takes a clear position: this industry profits from losses that fall hardest on young men and on households that can least afford them. Because it takes a side, the facts have to be airtight. Every item carries a label. Confirmed means a primary record backs it: a filing, a court document, a regulator’s action. Reported means credible but not yet verifiable. Disputed means the subject publicly denies it. Only confirmed items go into the ledger of who paid whom.

  • Charged isn’t convicted. People are described as charged or accused until a plea or a verdict.
  • Lawsuits are allegations. Sums sought are claims, not judgments.
  • No shaky numbers. When sources disagree on a figure, it’s left out.
  • Corrections in place. With the date and what changed.

It also publishes its own blind spots: sites that block automated readers, paywalls that show only a headline, small local outlets it under-covers. Its first audit found four stories six to ten weeks late; late finds keep their true date and are flagged in that day’s report.

Live

it keeps itself current

A scheduled Claude task checks every hour, around the clock, without anyone prompting it. It looks at primary sources first (federal campaign filings, lobbying disclosures, the Justice Department, courts, regulators, company investor pages), then the wire, national and trade press, reads each candidate at its source, labels it and republishes the page. The first check each morning also rereads every primary source and the comments for corrections. If nothing meets the bar, nothing gets posted.

  • Live status. The masthead shows when it last checked.
  • A running tab. What Americans have lost to sportsbooks since you arrived, at 2025’s pace.
  • Comments on every post. Through GitHub Discussions; readers can flag what it got wrong.
  • Nothing deleted. Posts move to a monthly archive after 90 days, and every run is committed to GitHub.
  • Take the data. Interactive charts, filters, and a ledger that copies out as a spreadsheet.
  • Free to reuse. The content and data are open (CC BY 4.0); the code is MIT.
Kit

a kit for the next one

Powerful institutions count on the public record being scattered and easy to forget. Bankrolled is my attempt at using AI for public good: gather what’s already public, check it against primary sources, and keep it in one searchable, permanent place.

Gambling is only the first subject. The Tracker Kit packages the method (sourced facts, honest labels, nothing deleted, an hourly AI run) so anyone can start a tracker on something else in about ten minutes. It comes with starting points for corporate lobbying, environmental disasters and conflict accountability.

If gambling is causing problems for you or someone you know, the National Problem Gambling Helpline is 1-800-GAMBLER (call or text).
