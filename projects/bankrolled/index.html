Bankrolled

A sourced record of the money behind American gambling, from sportsbooks to prediction markets: who pays whom, who loses, and who writes the rules.

read it github
Why

why gambling

Bankrolled is the first tracker built with Paper Trail. I started with gambling because I can’t think of another industry that went from illegal to everywhere so fast.

A few years ago sports betting was illegal in most states. Now it’s an app on everyone’s phone, and prediction markets like Kalshi and Polymarket sell the same bets as “contracts”. Bettors lost $17 billion to legal sportsbooks in 2025, nearly four times what they lost in 2021. The companies spend over a billion a year on marketing, sign deals with leagues and star athletes, and funded a $72 million super PAC before the 2026 midterms. Bankrolled keeps all of it in one place: the political money, the deals, the fines and lawsuits, and what the research says it costs people.

Rules

the rules

Bankrolled has a point of view: this industry makes its money from losses that land hardest on young men and on families who can least afford them. That’s why the facts have to hold up. Every item is labeled. Confirmed means an original record backs it, like a filing, a court document or a regulator’s action. Reported means credible but not yet verified. Disputed means the subject denies it. Only confirmed items go into the ledger.

  • Charged isn’t convicted. People are described as charged or accused until a plea or a verdict.
  • Lawsuits are allegations. Sums sought are claims, not judgments.
  • No shaky numbers. When sources disagree on a figure, it’s left out.
  • Corrections in place. With the date and what changed.

It also lists its blind spots, like sites that block automated readers, paywalls, and small local outlets it misses. Its first audit found four stories six to ten weeks late. Those keep their real date and get flagged in that day’s report.

Live

how it stays current

A scheduled Claude task checks every hour, day and night. It starts with the original sources (campaign filings, lobbying disclosures, the Justice Department, courts, regulators, company investor pages), then the news. It reads each story at its source, labels it and updates the page. The first check each morning also rereads every source and the comments, looking for corrections. If nothing new holds up, nothing gets posted.

  • Live status. The masthead shows when it last checked.
  • A running tab. What Americans have lost to sportsbooks since you arrived, at 2025’s pace.
  • Comments on every post. Through GitHub Discussions; readers can flag what it got wrong.
  • Nothing deleted. Posts move to a monthly archive after 90 days, and every run is committed to GitHub.
  • Take the data. Interactive charts, filters, and a ledger that copies out as a spreadsheet.
  • Free to reuse. The content and data are open (CC BY 4.0); the code is MIT.
