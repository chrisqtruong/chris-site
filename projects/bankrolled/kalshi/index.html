Following the money: Kalshi

Kalshi ads show up every time I open an app. So I followed the money: where it came from, how Kalshi makes it, and what it spends to win over regulators, politicians and young customers.

full research, with charts bankrolled
How

made with claude

I researched and wrote this with Claude, Anthropic’s AI assistant. Claude pulled the public records (lobbying disclosures, campaign filings, court dockets, regulator releases) and the reporting, drafted the text and drew the charts. I picked the subject, steered the work and checked it before publishing. AI can get things wrong, so every number in the full research page links to its source. Facts are as of October 7, 2026.

In

the money in

Kalshi didn’t come from gambling. Its founders were quant traders who met at MIT, and in 2020 they got something no one else had: a federal license to run an exchange for bets on events. That license is still the whole business. It’s what Kalshi points to when states say it’s running an illegal sportsbook.

The big money came after sports contracts launched in January 2025. Kalshi has raised about $3.6 billion from Sequoia, Paradigm, Andreessen Horowitz, Coatue and others, and its valuation went from $2 billion to $22 billion in under a year.

One detail stuck with me: Donald Trump Jr. has been a paid adviser since January 2025, and the Financial Times reported, citing unnamed sources, that he got about $300,000 in Kalshi stock without putting in any money.

Made

how it makes money

Kalshi takes a small fee on almost every trade, and almost all of that fee money comes from sports.

89%of Kalshi’s estimated 2025 fee revenue came from sports
$23.8Btraded on Kalshi in 2025, about twelve times 2024
−20%what traders lost on average before fees, in a study of 314,000 contract prices

That last number is from economists at University College Dublin. People buying long shots priced at 10 cents or less lost more than 60%. It works like a sportsbook, except Kalshi lets you start at 18, while most state sportsbooks make you wait until 21.

Out

buying influence

Most coverage says Kalshi started lobbying in July 2025. The Senate lobbying records show it was paying outside lobbyists back in December 2021. Spending has climbed every year since, and the first half of 2026 alone beat all of 2025.

  • Both parties’ insiders. Former aides to Chuck Schumer, a former Senate Republican floor staffer, a former senator who oversaw the CFTC, and the finance chair of Trump’s second inaugural committee.
  • The founders’ checks. About $1 million to candidates and party committees since 2025, roughly $615,000 to Republicans and $377,000 to Democrats.
  • The people suing it. In the first half of 2026 Kalshi gave $567,500 to the national associations that back both parties’ governors and attorneys general. Attorneys general in many states are suing Kalshi.
  • The states. Registered lobbyists in 41 states by September 2026, nearly all hired since April.
Law

the fight, and maryland

At least 17 states are fighting Kalshi, and Nevada, Michigan and Washington have forced it to stop sports contracts. Federal appeals courts have ruled against it three times and for it once, and New Jersey has asked the Supreme Court to settle it. Meanwhile the CFTC, which fought Kalshi until 2025, is now suing states on its side.

Here in Maryland, the state ordered Kalshi to stop in April 2025, but agreed to hold off until the federal appeals court rules. That case was argued in May and is still waiting on a decision. In August, Baltimore City sued Kalshi, Robinhood, Webull and Coinbase under its consumer protection law. Those are allegations for now.

Cost

who pays

No study measures harm among prediction-market users yet. But sports are most of Kalshi’s business, and the research on online sports betting is clear enough: peer-reviewed studies link it to lower credit scores, more debt, more calls to problem-gambling helplines, and more suicides among young, unmarried men. Health records show gambling-disorder diagnoses up about 60% since 2018 in states with legal betting, and down in states without it.

To be fair to the other side: Federal Reserve economists found Kalshi’s prices forecast Fed decisions about as well as professional surveys. That value comes from the economics and politics markets, though, and most of the money is in sports.
