Weather, in dots

Eighty-six years of weather for any place on earth, one dot at a time. Pick a city and a measure, then zoom from a single afternoon out to a whole decade.

Try it GitHub

Why

calm to look at, honest underneath

I liked the dots I’d been drawing for my own site and wanted to see what they could hold. Weather turned out to be a good fit: every hour since 1940 has been rebuilt from stations, balloons, ships and satellites, and it’s free for anyone to use.

Laid out week by week, the seasons look like weather. The changes underneath look like climate: Houston’s summers stretching into October, and the warm years piling up since 2010. Those changes reach everyone, but not equally, and I wanted something that makes them easy to see.

How

twelve measures, five sizes of dot

Temperature, feels like, humidity, dew point, rain, snow, cloud, wind, gusts, pressure, sunlight and sunshine, each with its own colour ramp. A dot can be an hour, a day, a week, a month or a year. A radar view draws the last two hours of rain as dots on a labelled map, looping like a doppler.

History comes from ERA5, the European weather centre’s reanalysis, served free by Open-Meteo. Everything the page fetches is saved in your browser, so a place you’ve opened before loads instantly.
