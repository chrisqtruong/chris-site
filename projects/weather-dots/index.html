Weather, in dots

Eighty-six years of weather for any place on earth, and how far it has drifted from normal. Pick a city and a measure, then zoom from a single afternoon out to the whole record.

try it github
Why

why weather

I liked the dots I’d been drawing for my own site and wanted to see what they could hold. Weather turned out to be a good fit: every hour since 1940 has been rebuilt from stations, balloons, ships and satellites, and it’s free for anyone to use.

Laid out week by week, the seasons look like weather. The changes underneath look like climate: Houston’s summers stretching into October, and the warm years piling up since 2010. Those changes reach everyone, but not equally, and I wanted something that makes them easy to see.

Change

against its own normal

Switch to change and each cell is colored by how far it sat from the same time of year in 1951–1980, the baseline NASA uses for its global record. Laid out by year, the cells become warming stripes. The page also says it in one line: Houston’s last ten years ran 1.7°F warmer than that normal.

A thin line across the top of the page holds the whole planet, every year since 1850, from NOAA. Hover over it to read any year.

How

twelve measures, dots or field

Temperature, feels like, humidity, dew point, rain, snow, cloud, wind, gusts, pressure, sunlight and sunshine, each with its own color ramp. A dot can be an hour, a day, a week, a month or a year. Field melts the dots into one soft, slowly drifting surface with a fine print grain, so slow patterns show through the noise. Quiet, on by default, puts the controls away so the picture fills the page; you can still hover to read it. Save image turns whatever you’re looking at into a print-size picture, signed small in the corner. A radar view draws the last two hours of rain as dots on a labeled map, looping like a doppler.

History comes from ERA5, the European weather center’s reanalysis, served free by Open-Meteo. Everything the page fetches is saved in your browser, so a place you’ve opened before loads instantly.
