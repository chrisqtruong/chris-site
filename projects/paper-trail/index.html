Paper Trail

A kit I built for keeping a public record of how big industries affect people’s lives, with a source for every fact. It runs two trackers so far: Bankrolled, on gambling, and Tracewire, on tech, AI and data centers.

start your own see the trackers
Why

where this came from

I’m always impressed by independent projects that keep track of corruption and corporate greed, usually because one person decided somebody had to. One I think about a lot is Rachel LaPointe’s Gendered Police Violence, where a New York social worker documents gender-based violence by police, one case at a time. It made me want to start my own.

Most of this information is already public. It’s just spread out: a campaign filing here, a court ruling there, a fine announced late on a Friday. That makes it easy to miss and easy to forget.

Paper Trail is my attempt at putting AI to good use. It collects what’s public, checks it against the original documents, and keeps it in one place anyone can search and reuse.

How

how it works

Each tracker runs by itself, every hour.

  1. Gather. A small program polls public feeds every few minutes (regulators, courts, government filings, newsrooms) and posts what it finds as unverified headlines, clearly labeled.
  2. Verify. Every hour, a scheduled Claude run reads primary sources first, opens each candidate at its source, and labels it confirmed, reported or disputed. Only confirmed items enter the ledger of who paid whom.
  3. Keep. Every source link gets a saved copy on the Internet Archive, older posts move to a monthly archive, and every change is committed to GitHub. Nothing is ever deleted.
  4. Show. One fast static page per tracker: a feed, the ledger, charts, entities, comments, and a status page that says what was checked and what couldn’t be read.

Tracewire runs all four steps. Bankrolled, the first tracker, came before the live headline feed and saved copies; it gathers, verifies, archives and shows through its hourly run.

Rules

the rules

The trackers have a point of view, so the facts need to hold up. They all follow the same rules.

  • Three labels. Confirmed, reported or disputed, on every item, so you always know how sure it is.
  • Accused until convicted. Lawsuits are allegations; it never calls anything corruption in its own voice, only what was filed, charged or reported, and by whom.
  • No shaky numbers. When sources disagree on a figure, it’s left out.
  • Mistakes stay visible. Corrections are made in place, late finds keep their true date, and each tracker publishes its own blind spots.

Yours

start your own

The kit is called the Tracker Kit. One command turns a short settings file into a working tracker, ready to put on GitHub, with starting points for corporate lobbying, environmental disasters and conflict accountability.

Each tracker lives in its own repository, so a mistake in one can’t spill into another, and any of them can be handed off. How a tracker behaves is written in plain text files it rereads every run, so changing it means editing a document, not code.
