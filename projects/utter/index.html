Why

my own dictation, on my own phone

I was inspired by other dictation apps, like SpeakType on the Mac, and wanted to make my own that runs entirely on the phone: no account, no server, nothing leaving it.

It’s made for longer thoughts than built-in dictation: monologues, ideas on a walk, notes I want to keep.

It’s free, with no account and no ads, and I plan to keep it that way for as long as I can. Your recordings, notes and data stay on your own phone, and the code is open for anyone to use, change and build on.

Details

a few things it does

it tells you how sure it is

Every note gets a match score, and words the model wasn’t sure about get a dotted underline, so you know where to look before you send it.

reads like notes

A pause at the end of a sentence starts a new paragraph, ums and uhs are gone, and one tap tidies the rest, with Apple Intelligence or a small open model like Qwen, still on the phone.

private by default

Speech becomes text on the phone, with NVIDIA’s Parakeet or OpenAI’s Whisper, and the recording is thrown away as soon as it’s done.

Study

a study in the small stuff

Utter is also my own study in usability. I never formally learned UI/UX, but after years of being a user, I wanted to think through the tiny details myself and keep asking what would feel obvious. A few that came out of it:

buttons only when they’d help

Utter quietly checks whether tidying would change anything, and only shows the button if it would. Undo always goes back to exactly what you said.

soft and hard actions feel different

Archiving is one swipe and easy to undo. Deleting can’t be undone, so it always asks first.

hold to start, slide to pick many

Press and hold a note to start selecting, then slide down the circles to pick a whole run at once, instead of hunting for a Select button first.

Next

on the roadmap

  1. iOS keyboard. It’s a real keyboard now: full keys for quick fixes, the mic up top, and suggestions from the phone’s own spell checker. iOS keeps the microphone away from keyboards, so the app listens in the background and hands the text back. Next: accents and a smoother first hand-off.
  2. A plain privacy page. One calm page, in the app and here, saying exactly what stays on the phone. (Everything.)
